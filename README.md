# Bible Family Tree

An interactive timeline of every named person in the Bible, from Adam to the apostles. Zoom and drag along the years, hover over a person to see lines to their family, and open a person's window for their dates, relatives and key scriptures.

The ⓘ button opens **About this timeline**, a short guide for visitors: moving around, opening a person, who is shown, how to read the three kinds of date, the map and the sources. It is written for people using the site, so it names no files or code; the text is in `index.html` (English) and `js/i18n.js` (Chinese, `about.*` keys).

The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. There is no build step and nothing to install to view it.

## Run it locally

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then open http://localhost:8000
```

## Logo

The logo is a small family tree in line art: a trunk on a horizon line (a nod to the timeline), two branches that split again, and gold dots for the people at each tip. The header copy is inline SVG in `index.html` (`.brand-mark`, styled in `css/style.css`); the browser tab uses the same tree, simplified for 16px, from `img/favicon.svg`. The tab icon has no background circle so the tree fills the space: it is bright gold when the browser is in dark mode and a deeper gold in light mode (a `prefers-color-scheme` rule inside the SVG), so it stays visible on any tab bar.

## First visit and search

The opening view fits the Bible's years, and the key figures already show there (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, Jesus and the rest, as many as fit); more people appear as you zoom in.

On a first visit a **Start here** card sits above the timeline in place of the zoom hint: click or tap a name, zoom and drag, the Books and People buttons, and search. **Got it** (or Escape) hides it for good (`bt-welcome` in the browser). A shared link opens straight to what was shared, without the card.

**Search** finds, in this order: a year ("1000 BCE", "33 CE", 公元前1000; a bare number offers both), a verse ("Dan 9:25", "1 Sam 17", 但以理書9:25; a book name may be shortened), people by name, Bible books, major events, places, people by role, then activities. A verse lists every event and activity on the timeline that cites it, plus a link to read it on wol.jw.org; a place lists the activities that happened there. Picking a year marks it with the cursor line; picking an activity opens that person's window at it. Rows from history are in italics. The citation parsing is shared with the scripture links (`Scripture.cites()` and `Scripture.query()` in `js/scripture.js`).

## People the Bible does not name

The people list is built from the person articles in *Insight on the Scriptures*, and Insight writes an article only for a name. So people the Bible never names were missing, and their families had gaps: Ammon (Ben-ammi) had no mother. `data/source/unnamed-people.json` adds 34 of them, in the same shape as the Insight records, with a label for a name ("Lot's younger daughter", "Noah's wife", "Pharaoh of the Exodus") and the verses that mention them. A person is included when they have a clear family link to someone on the timeline (the wives of Cain, Noah, his sons, Lot, Potiphar, Manoah, Job, Jeroboam, Isaiah, Ezekiel, Peter and Pilate; Lot's daughters; Jephthah's and Jairus' daughters; Paul's sister and nephew) or a notable part in a Bible account (the Pharaohs of Joseph's day and of the Exodus, the Pharaoh's daughter who adopted Moses, the queen of Sheba, the widow of Zarephath, the Israelite girl in Naaman's house, the medium of En-dor, the Samaritan woman, the poor widow, the evildoer beside Jesus, the Ethiopian eunuch). Anonymous crowds, unnamed children and passing mentions are left out. A few unnamed people already had an Insight article and were there before (the Shunammite woman). Their windows cite only the Bible, not *Insight*.

## Era names

The names of the eras (Before the Flood, The Judges and so on) sit at the top of the screen. On a computer a name shows when it fits inside its era's band. Phones are too narrow for that, so there the names stack left to right in up to three rows at the top, and the people start a little lower to make room. The rows are `ERA_ROWS` in `js/app.js`. A dotted line runs down the screen at each boundary between eras, and only there: a major event inside an era (such as Solomon's Temple Completed in the United Kingdom) has its dot and name on the timeline but no dotted line, so the lines always match the eras in `TIMELINE_ERAS` in `data/events.js`.

## Event labels

Major event names sit in rows under the axis: three rows on a computer, two on a phone (`evRows` in `layout()` in `js/app.js`). Each label hangs from its dot by a straight stem, and the layout never lets a stem run through another label: a label may sit centred on its stem or slide to one side, and it takes a lower row only when the rows above are clear at its stem. A label that has no clear spot stays hidden until you zoom in; its dot still shows the details on hover or tap.

A major event's details open in a tooltip above the axis: point at the event on a computer, or tap it on a phone (tap it again to zoom in). Its scripture citation links to the passage on wol.jw.org and, when the event has a known place, a Google Maps link sits under its locator map. On a computer the tooltip stays open while the pointer moves from the event up into it (`hideTipSoon()` in `js/app.js` gives a short grace delay) and closes once the pointer has left both; on a phone it stays until you tap somewhere else. Labels have a thin outline in the page colour, so dotted lines behind them are cut cleanly.

The personal lifespan tracks (hover and open windows) start below the event label rows, so they never cover an event name.

## Portraits

Each person's lifespan line, solid or dotted, starts just after their name, and it also breaks wherever another person's name sits on it (a short-lived person's name may share a lane with a long line). So no name ever sits on top of a line. The gaps are sized to the measured name width (re-measured once the web fonts load); see `cutLifeLines()` in `js/app.js`.

Hovering (or tapping) a person shows their line-drawn portrait in a dark card, always in the same spot: under the lifespan tracks, between the timeline and the bottom controls, so it never sits on top of the names. The card moves down a little as open windows add tracks, and stops above the minimap and the window dock. When several people are open and there is no room left for the drawing, the card shrinks to just the name and dates, the tracks move a little closer together, and the hovered person's event notes are left out, so every lifespan line stays visible. Only if even that is not enough is the oldest open person's track left out (their window stays in the dock). The placement is `placePortrait()` and `trackFit()` in `js/app.js`.

Portraits are drawn by people group. There are 50 designs for men and 26 for women for Bible times (plus one Greek royal design and a set for people after the Bible) (listed as `PRESETS` in `js/portrait.js`), each tagged with the peoples it fits: Israelites (headbands, caps, the short Lachish turban, long or curly hair, braids), Egyptians (shaved heads, short or plaited wigs, broad collars, a pharaoh's striped headdress), Philistines (feathered headdress), Assyrians and Babylonians (long curled hair, tiered beards, tall caps, pointed helmets), Persians and Medes (fluted or rounded caps, a crenellated royal crown), Romans (cropped hair, clean-shaven, an officer's crested helmet, an emperor's laurel wreath), Greeks and the Herods, Canaanites and Phoenicians, Arameans, Edom, Moab and Ammon, desert peoples such as the Midianites, and Cushites. There are no draped head cloths, veils or hair buns. The looks follow Insight's articles on dress, headdress, hair and beards, the ancient reliefs they cite, and the illustrations in the *Lessons You Can Learn From the Bible* book on wol.jw.org; the comment above `PRESETS` lists the verses. A person's people comes from the nation or tribe Insight gives (the `group` field), or from words in their role and summary; a `look.culture` hint in `data/characters.js` overrides it (Joseph is drawn as an Egyptian official, Nimrod as a Mesopotamian king). Each person gets one design of their own people, picked once for the whole list: people are taken in birth order, each gets the design used longest ago, and a design a parent, child, sibling or spouse already has is skipped. Key figures are picked first among themselves, so neighbouring key figures also differ in headwear. A person's own `look` hints still set age, beard and any hair the Bible mentions (Samson, Samuel, Absalom); a priest's turban always wins, and a crowned king, or a foreign ruler whose role says so ("King of Assyria", "Pharaoh of Egypt", "Roman emperor"), wears his own people's royal headwear. Small differences are then drawn from each person's id, so two people with the same design never look exactly alike.

People from history (the squares) use the same system. Greek kings after Alexander wear the plain ribbon diadem of their coins (the "Hellenistic king's diadem" royal design), and the Hasmonaeans and other Jews of that time are drawn as Israelites (`look.culture`). The 17 well-known people after the Bible each have a design of their own, named by `look.design` in `data/source/history-people.json`, with headwear and dress of their own era: the late Roman emperors' pearl diadem and brooched cloak (Constantine, Theodosius I), a bishop's pallium (Athanasius, Leo I), Jerome as a bald, bearded monk, Urban II's mitre, Peter Waldo's felt cap, scholars' black skullcaps and fur-collared gowns (Wycliffe, Tyndale, Calvin), Hus with a pointed beard, Gutenberg's fur hat, Mehmed II's great round turban, Luther's doctor's cap, Henry VIII's Tudor flat cap with a feather, and nineteenth-century coats and neckties for Russell (white beard) and Rutherford (clean-shaven, round glasses). They follow the period portraits, statues and photographs that the Watchtower publications on wol.jw.org reproduce in their articles on these people: *Mankind's Search for God* ch. 11-13 (Wycliffe, Hus, Luther, Calvin), *The Watchtower* on Jerome (2009, statue in Bethlehem), Tyndale (1995, Hertford College portrait), Luther (2003), Calvin (2010) and Constantine, *Awake!* on Gutenberg (1998) and the Byzantine Empire (2001), and *Jehovah's Witnesses—Proclaimers of God's Kingdom* ch. 6 (Russell and Rutherford photographs). Muhammad and the first leaders of Islam are not drawn: Muslims hold that Muhammad must not be pictured, and *Mankind's Search for God* ch. 12 shows only places, never him. The new headwear (`pearls`, `skullcap`, `doctorcap`, `flatcap`, `furhat`, `mitre`, `kavuk`), dress (`L.dress`: `gown`, `coat`, `pallium`, `cloak`) and `glasses` are drawn in `build()`.

The Governing Body members and Maria Russell are drawn too. The members wear a modern suit and tie (the "Suit and tie" design, `dress: "suit"`), and each one's `look` in `data/source/history-people.json` sets what tells them apart: hair combed back (`swept`), parted (`parted`), thinning (`receding`) or bald, grey or white hair (`grey`, drawn in outline rather than filled), round, rectangular or rimless glasses (`glasses`, `specs`, `rimless`), a moustache (`beard: "moustache"`), the tie's pattern (`stripes`, `dots`), a vest or pocket square, and the face and mouth. These follow the photographs in the Watchtower publications on wol.jw.org, which are drawn from and not reproduced: Nathan Knorr and Lloyd Barry (*God's Kingdom Rules!*), Frederick Franz (*The Watchtower*, 2023), Theodore Jaracz and Albert Schroeder (*The Watchtower*, 2018 and 2017), John Barr and David Splane (David Splane's life story, 2026), Samuel Herd and Stephen Lett (Samuel Herd's life story, 2018), Gerrit Lösch, Guy Pierce, Anthony Morris and Geoffrey Jackson (their life stories and obituary, 2014-2015), and Mark Sanderson, Kenneth Cook, Gage Fleegle, Jeffrey Winder, Jody Jedele and Jacob Rumph (the announcements of new members, 2013-2025). ## Personal timelines and colours

Each open window adds that person's lifespan as a track under the timeline. The small dots on a track are the person's major activities: hover one (or tap it on a phone) to see its date and text, and click it (or tap it again) to open the person's window scrolled to that activity, which is briefly highlighted. It works the other way too: clicking an activity in a window briefly highlights its dot on the window's own lifespan bar, which stays pinned at the top of the window while you scroll through the activities.

While you hover a person (or tap them on a phone), their activity dots also appear on their own lifespan line in the main timeline, in gold, and go away when the pointer leaves; they work like the track dots. Dots that would fall under the person's name are left out there (they are still on the track). They are drawn in their own layer above the names (`gLifeActs`, at the end of `renderTracks()` in `js/app.js`), so a dot can be pointed at even where a name's hover area reaches.

A window's **Companions** row, the last row of the Family list (label on the left, names on the right), lists the people who served, traveled or stood with that person: Elijah and Elisha, Elisha and Gehazi, David and Jonathan, Daniel and his three friends, Job's three companions, Jesus and the apostles, Paul and his fellow workers (26 in all). The groups are in `data/source/companions.json`, each with the verses that show it; `node tools/build-people.mjs` gives everyone in a group the others (the `comp` field). Each chip's tooltip names its verses, all of them are under **Verses**, and family listed above is left out. The groups were found by checking every person's *Insight* relations and summary for companions, attendants, fellow workers and fellow travelers, then the Bible accounts. Apostle Philip, Eliphaz, Zophar, Joanna, Susanna, Epaphras and Epaphroditus were missing from the extracted *Insight* list and are in `data/source/added-people.json`.

When a key figure has no family links, their window shows the hand-written `family` line from `data/characters.js` instead; its `links` (person ids) make the names in it open those people (Daniel's companions Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, Ezra's ancestor Aaron, Barnabas' cousin Mark). A name is found by the person's name or other names, in English or Chinese.

Every person's window has a **Contemporaries** list under Family: the key figures whose lifespans overlap theirs (Joshua, Caleb and Jethro in Moses' window; John, Timothy and Luke in Onesimus' window), family left out since it is listed just above, and the people who shared the most years first. It is folded by default, so it adds one line until opened, and shows the first 8 names with a **Show all** button for longer lists (Paul has 29). Click a name to open that person. Most of these people have assumed dates placed by their generation, so the list is a guide, not a fact; their name chips have a dashed border and their tooltip says the overlap is approximate. Only key figures are in the list, since a list from all 2,800 people would be hundreds of names long (Moses overlaps 299 people) and almost all on placeholder dates. Two fallbacks keep the list from ever being missing: someone no key figure lived alongside (Abel, a few from history) gets everyone who did, and someone nobody else on the timeline lived alongside (Urban II, Peter Waldo) gets the 4 people who lived nearest in time, with a note saying so and no count. The code is `contemporariesHTML()` in `js/app.js`.

Family links (the dashed curves shown while hovering a person) are coloured by sex at each end: blue for a man, rose for a woman, blending from one to the other along the line, and both the hovered person's dot and each relative's dot get a ring in their colour. So a father's links to his sons end in blue and to his daughters in rose. The 16 Insight entries whose sex is unknown (`gender: "u"`, for example Gog and Junias) keep a gold end. The two colours are `--male` and `--female` in `css/style.css`.

Gold marks people you are only hovering over. Every opened person gets their own colour (sea green, terracotta, lavender, sage, ivory; no blue or rose, since those mean man and woman on the family links), used for their window, track and ring on the timeline, so an opened person never looks the same as one that is not opened. The palette is `COLORS` in `js/app.js`.

## Bible books band

The book button in the top bar (next to the People button) shows the 66 books of the Bible as lines just above the main line; the people move up to make room. Books are drawn in sage green (`--book` in `css/style.css`) so they stand apart from the cream lifespans and from the gold used for whatever is hovered or picked; a hovered or opened book turns gold like everything else. The book button turns sage too while the books are shown, so it reads as their switch. On short screens the main line moves down as far as it needs to so the top people stay on screen (keeping room for at least three opened lifespans under it), and where that is not enough the band uses fewer rows, with the books that don't fit in its "+N" marks. The choice is remembered in the browser (`bt-books`) and kept in shared links (`&b=1`).

- **Lines:** each book spans the years it covers, from the *New World Translation* "Table of the Books of the Bible" (writer, place written, writing completed, time covered). The table gives one span for 1 and 2 Kings together and one for 1 and 2 Chronicles together, so each pair is drawn as one line ("1 Kings · 2 Kings"). A book the table gives no span for runs between its events (Ruth, Jonah, Malachi), or is just a mark at the year its writing was completed (Psalms, Proverbs, the letters).
- **Completed:** a hollow diamond (drawn under the event dots, so a dot in the same year sits inside it) marks the year the writing was completed (the table's "writing completed", `done`). Where that is later than the end of the span (Genesis, Judges, Chronicles, Ezra, the Gospels), a dotted tail runs from the line's end to the diamond; otherwise the diamond is the line's end. The tail counts as part of the book when the rows are packed.
- **Several writers:** 1 Samuel, Psalms and Proverbs list who wrote which part (`parts`) and who finished the book long after its first writer died (`by`), from *"All Scripture Is Inspired of God and Beneficial"* (Bible Book Numbers 9, 19, 20) and the superscriptions of the Psalms. Every other book's writers were alive in its completion year.
- **From a writer's window:** "Bible books written" lists every book the person wrote or had a part in (from `w`, `parts` and `by`); clicking one turns the band on, zooms to the book and opens its card beside the window.
- **Rows:** seven rows on a computer, six on a phone (`bookRows` in `layout()`), filled nearest the main line first in book order. No book is ever left out: a book with no room for its name is drawn as its line alone (its name is on its card), and where even that has no room, a small "+3" chip stands for the books there; its card lists them (each opens its own card, zoomed in) and clicking the chip zooms in on them. A name whose line starts off the left edge stays at the edge; when there is no room for a name before its line (Acts begins where the four Gospels end), it goes after the line.
- **Dots:** each book's main events, drawn like the activity dots on an open lifespan. Hover (or tap) for the activity, click (or tap again) to open it in the person's window. A dot that is a major event (for example the Exodus) shows the event's tooltip and zooms to it.
- **Card:** hovering a book name (or tapping it) shows its card: for the books the handbook *God's Word Is Truth* covers so far, a line on the book's main subject; then the writer (click to open their window), where and when it was written, the time covered, its main events (each clickable), a link to read the book on wol.jw.org and, where there is one, a link to the book's chapter in *God's Word Is Truth*. When the card is short of room (on a phone it sits under the main line), everything between the title and the links scrolls. The card sits above the band (or under the main line when there is too little room there), so it never covers a book's line or dots; a long list of events scrolls inside it. Click the book (or tap again) to open it as a window.
- **Window:** a book's window works like a person's (`bookWindowHTML()` and `wireBookWindow()` in `js/app.js`): it can be dragged, folded into the dock and closed, and has the same star, locate, link, fold and close buttons (`winBtns()`). It shows the card's details, a **My notes** box and the main events; hovering an event lights up its dot on the band (`setBookHL()`), and clicking one opens it. Locate turns the band on and zooms to the book. A book's id is `book-<Bible book number>` (Genesis `book-1`, Psalms `book-19`), used in notes and links: `#p=book-19` opens the Psalms window with the band on. Book windows do not get a lifespan track or move the Places map.

The data is in `data/books.js` (hand-editable). `focus` is the subject line in English and Chinese, written for this site from the handbook's chapter, and `gwt` is that chapter's document number on wol.jw.org. A dot is either `"person-id:n"`, the person's n-th activity in `data/people.js` (counting from 0, before the activities are sorted), or `"e:event-id"` from `data/events.js`, so its text and scripture come from there in both languages.

## My notes and bookmarks

The star at the top of a person's or a book's window bookmarks them, and the **My notes** box in the window keeps the visitor's own notes (saved as they type). Both are stored only in that browser (`localStorage` key `bt-notes`, `{ id: { s: starred, n: note, t: last change } }`); nothing is sent anywhere, and clearing the browser's data removes them.

- **My bookmarks** in the People menu shows only the starred people (it follows the same zoom rule as the other modes), and on the books band only the starred books.
- **My notes and bookmarks** in the People menu lists everyone with a star or a note in timeline order, then the books in the order they were completed; click a name to open their window. **Download** saves them as a text file, **Load** reads such a file back in (to move notes to another device or restore them), and **Print** prints just the list.
- **The file:** a readable list, then one `BFT-NOTES {…}` line with the same notes as data, which is what Load reads. Loading merges: a bookmark on either side stays, and when both sides have different notes for a person both texts are kept, the newer first, so nothing is lost.

## Places map

On a computer, a **Places** map window opens on the left beside the person windows. It shows the places of the window in use: click another person's window and the map switches to them. Its minimize button folds it to a "Map" chip in the dock, and the chip brings it back. Each place has a numbered pin in the order of the person's life, and a dashed line joins them. The places are part of **Major activities**: an activity that happened somewhere carries a small place tag with its pin number, and the places the Bible names that were not already activities (Isaac in Gerar, Paul at Paphos) are added to that list, so there is one list. Point at an activity or a pin and the pin lights up, with the place's name, how sure the site is and the reason under the map. The line under the map keeps the last place pointed at (tapped, on a phone) and ends with a link that opens that spot in Google Maps, satellite view, in a new tab (the Google Maps app on a phone); it reads "Open in Google Maps" for a certain site and just "Google Maps" for a likely or uncertain one, since there the pin is the proposed spot, not a certain one. Click a pin to scroll the window to the activities there, and click a place tag to see it on the map. Places closer together than a pin (Jericho and Gilgal; Hebron, Mamre and the cave of Machpelah) are pushed apart just enough that each keeps its own numbered pin and name, in the same direction from its neighbours, with a fine line back to a small dot where the place really is; a name with no room beside its pin sits a little farther out on a fine line. When a life ranges beyond the land, a switch picks between the first map and a closer map of the land. On a long journey, each region farther off where the pins crowd gets its own map too (Paul: Cyprus + Syria, Judea, Galatia, Asia, Macedonia and Achaia; two places are enough when the journey has ten or more), named for the lands or Roman provinces the Bible uses (Acts; *NWT* maps B13 and B14), with up to two joined by +. The places are grouped by joining the closest ones first, as long as a group still fits a region about 400 km by 290 km, so each map is one area on the ground. Each small area where places crowd (two pins closer than about a tenth of the map) gets its own close-up (Jesus has one for Judea and one for Galilee), and inside a close-up, places only a few km apart get a closer map still (Jerusalem: Jerusalem, the Mount of Olives, Bethany and Bethlehem; Capernaum and Bethsaida), up to eight maps per person, in the order the person first went there. A close-up is made only where it shows the places much closer than the map above it. On a wider map each closer map is a dashed box with its name, which opens it (the name sits just above, below or beside its box, clear of the other boxes, with an arrow pointing to the box; where there is no room it is shortened to its first land, or left to the switch); a region map that would add only a place or two to the close-up is left out, so Joshua's Canaan map is just Jericho to Shechem and Kadesh stays on the first map. The first map is **Overall** for everyone; its full name is Whole journey for a life that went far from home (Abraham from Ur, Paul to Rome) and All places otherwise. The names are kept short so the switch stays on one line; when its buttons still do not fit (Paul's seven maps), it slides sideways by touch, trackpad, mouse wheel or dragging with the mouse, a fade at an edge shows that more buttons lie that way, and it slides by itself to show the button of the map in view (`wireBar()` in js/app.js). Hovering a button shows its full name. The closer maps take the name the land had in the person's day: Canaan before the kings, Israel under Saul, David and Solomon, Israel and Judah after the split, and Galilee, Samaria and Judea in Jesus' day, joined with + (Galilee + Judea) (a close-up around Jerusalem is Judea); a map mostly outside the land before the kings (Moses at Kadesh and in Moab) is Near Canaan, and a close-up with no fitting name is named for the place visited most there (Cherith). The map turns by itself to follow the activities: pointing at an activity (tapping it on a phone) shows the closest map that has its place. The rule is `BibleMap.levels()` in js/map.js. The dots on a person's lifespan do the same: pointing at an activity dot on the timeline (while the person's window is open) or on the lifespan bar at the top of the window lights its place, with its distances, and turns the map to it, and on a computer the map window turns to that person; on a phone, where the window covers the lifespans, tapping a timeline dot twice opens the window at that activity with its place on the map (`pointAct()` and `wirePlaces()` in js/app.js). The map moves between its maps so the eye can follow: going to a closer map, the place (and the dashed box of the closer map) lights up on the map you are on, and the view then zooms into it (Abraham from 5 Egypt to 6 Hebron: Hebron's pin and the Canaan box light up on the Journey map, then the view zooms in to Hebron); going back out, the view zooms out and the box you came from and the new place light up; between two close-ups side by side (Galilee and Jerusalem) the view moves across. While a map is blown up its pins, names, lines and dashes stay their usual size on screen (they spread apart, as on a real map), so the zoom lands without a jump. It takes about half a second, a newer move cancels one still under way, and with the system's reduce-motion setting on, the map simply switches. The code is `setMapView()` in js/app.js. On a phone there is no room for a second window, so the map sits in the person's window and stays pinned under the lifespan bar while the activities scroll; the ▴ button (Hide map) folds it to give the list the room. Every major event with a known place also shows a small locator map in its tooltip. A dashed pin means the location is uncertain (its place tag ends in "?"). A person with only one place (Job in Uz, Noah at Ararat) gets a wider map so the place has its surroundings. People the Bible gives no locatable place for (Cain's land of Nod, most of those before the Flood) have no map.

**Landscape.** The maps show the main landscape, not every hill: mountain ranges as rows of small peaks (Lebanon, Anti-Lebanon, Taurus, Zagros, the hill country of Judah and Ephraim, Gilead, Mount Seir), single mountains as a small triangle (Hermon, Tabor, Gilboa, Ebal and Gerizim, Nebo, Sinai), deserts as a fine stipple (the Syrian and Arabian Deserts, the wildernesses of Shur, Paran, Sin and Judah, the Negeb), and the names of rivers, valleys and plains (Jordan Valley, Jezreel Valley, Plain of Sharon, Shephelah, Arabah) and highlands (Bashan, Gilead, Moab, Edom). Each feature shows only at a zoom where it helps: the whole Fertile Crescent shows the great ranges and deserts, a close-up of Galilee shows Mount Tabor and the Jezreel Valley, and a close-up of a few towns (around Jerusalem) shows none of the ranges. A feature never covers a pin or a name, and a mountain that is one of the person's places is shown only as its pin. The features are `data/terrain.js`, traced by hand from the *New World Translation* appendix maps (B2, B3, B4, B7, B10, B13), so they are approximate; `terrain()` in `js/map.js` draws them.

**Routes.** The line between two places follows the way people went, not a straight line. Each leg takes the shortest way along a network of the ancient roads drawn on the NWT appendix maps: the Euphrates road and the road through Syria (B2), the Way of the Sea and the King's Road (B2), the ridge road through the hill country, the Jordan Valley road and the Jericho road (B10), the Way of Beth-horon and the valleys to the coast (B4, B7), the Way to Shur and the Way to Mount Seir (B2, B3), and Paul's roads through Asia Minor, Macedonia and Greece (B13). Some legs are given whole: the Exodus follows B3's "Possible Route of Exodus" (Red Sea, Marah, Elim, Sinai, Kadesh, around Edom to the plains of Moab), and Paul's voyages follow B13 and the stops Acts names (Seleucia to Salamis, past Cnidus, Crete and Cauda to Malta, Syracuse, Rhegium and Puteoli). Where no source shows the way, the usual walking or sailing route of the region is used and marked "common route": along valleys and coasts, around the seas and the heads of the gulfs (Moses to Midian, Babylon to Susa, Dublin to London by Holyhead). **ORBIS.** For people from Alexander the Great on (the Greek and Roman world), a leg that leaves the land of Israel and that no Bible account describes takes the quickest summer route of [ORBIS](https://orbis.stanford.edu/), the Stanford Geospatial Network Model of the Roman World (W. Scheidel and E. Meeks): its roads, coastal and open-sea lanes and rivers, joined to the NWT map roads (Aquila and Priscilla from Rome to Corinth, Apollos from Alexandria, Mark to Babylon, Constantine to Nicaea). The Bible account always comes first: Paul's voyages keep the stops Acts names, and legs inside the land of Israel keep the B10 roads, which are more detailed there. ORBIS's network (its towns, ports and capes, and the days between them) is kept in `data/source/orbis/` (from the [gorbit](https://github.com/sfsheath/gorbit) copy of [orbis_v2](https://github.com/emeeks/orbis_v2), MIT, and the [Stanford Digital Repository](https://purl.stanford.edu/mn425tz9757), CC BY) and built into `data/orbis.js` by `node tools/build-orbis.mjs`. ORBIS draws a sea lane as a straight line from port to port, so the build bends each lane that would cross a cape or an island around it: `tools/sea-way.mjs` lays the map's coastline on a grid of about 2.5 km and finds a way across the water a little off the shore, keeping only the corners where it turns. The same tool (`node tools/sea-way.mjs lon,lat lon,lat`) gave the corners of the hand-written voyages, such as Paul past Cnidus and Salmone to Malta. A place off the roads (Mount Tabor, the Mount of Olives) is joined to the nearest road towns. A leg taken more than once is drawn once. **Distances.** When a place lights up (pointing at it or at one of its activities, tapping on a phone), each leg of the route from or to it turns solid and bright and shows its length beside it, in the route's colour, so each number can be matched to its line: for Abraham, Haran shows ~990 km to Ur and ~710 km on to Shechem. The length is measured along the drawn way, not as the crow flies, and rounded with a ~ since the routes are approximate (to half a km under 10 km, to the km under 30, to 5 km under 300, else to 10). A number sits near the middle of its leg where it covers no pin, name, route or closer map's box; a leg too short on a map for its number shows it on the closer map. The code is `distLabels()` in `js/map.js`. Names keep off the routes: a place name moves to another side of its pin, or farther out on a fine line (never nearer another pin than its own), and only crosses a route when no spot is free; names of seas, landscape and closer maps move a little or are left out, and the small mountain marks skip the route. The Google Maps directions service could not be used for the fallback, since it needs a paid API key that a site without a server cannot keep secret. The data is `data/routes.js` (hand-written: `ROADS`, waypoints in `ROUTE_WAY`, and whole legs in `TRAVELS`, each with its source) and `data/orbis.js` (generated); `BibleMap.way(from, to, roman)` in `js/map.js` finds the way and lists the source of each piece. Of the 325 legs on the maps, 218 follow the Bible accounts, the source maps and roads, 58 take ORBIS routes, 39 rely on common routes for a large part, and 10 are still straight lines over land where no road is known (Tema in Arabia, Mecca to Medina, places in Europe and America after the Bible).

The data is `data/places.js`, written by hand and easy to edit:

- `BIBLE_PLACES` lists each place with its longitude and latitude, how sure the location is (`known`, `likely`, `possible`) and a note on how it is identified.
- `PERSON_PLACES` lists each person's places in order. `["haran", 0]` ties activity 0 of that person in `data/characters.js` to Haran; `["ur", -2018, "Grows up in Ur of the Chaldeans", "Genesis 11:28, 31; Acts 7:2"]` adds a place that is not one of the activities; the site shows it as an activity too.
- `EVENT_PLACES` gives the place of each major event in `data/events.js`.

**Where the locations come from.** Which place goes with which activity comes from the scripture cited for it. The coordinates come from the [OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding Data](https://github.com/openbibleinfo/Bible-Geocoding-Data) (CC BY 4.0), which also scores how confident scholars are; each place keeps its OpenBible id (`ob`), so you can look it up at `https://www.openbible.info/geo/ancient/<ob>`. Disputed sites were checked against *Insight on the Scriptures* on wol.jw.org and the maps in the *New World Translation* (nwt_E.pdf: Appendix A7 maps 1 to 6 and maps B2, B3 and B10), and where they prefer another site, that site is used: Kadesh at ʽAin Qedeis, Mount Sinai at Ras Safsafa (traditional site), Mount Hor at Jebel Madurah, Samuel's Ramah at Rentis, Cana at Khirbet Qana, the Red Sea crossing near the north end of the Gulf of Suez, the transfiguration probably on Mount Hermon, and Sodom at the south end of the Salt Sea. Two places OpenBible gives no point for are placed from *Insight* alone and marked uncertain: the land of Uz (Job) east of the Promised Land near Edom, in northern Arabia, and Eden (Adam and Eve) at its traditional site just south of Lake Van. 78 of the 125 places are certain, 19 likely and 28 uncertain.

The base map (`data/basemap.js`, generated) is the Natural Earth 1:10m coastline (public domain) with the Jordan, Euphrates, Tigris and Nile, the Dead Sea and the Sea of Galilee from OpenBible.info (from OpenStreetMap). The coastline and lakes are today's, so the Dead Sea and the head of the Persian Gulf look as they do now, not as they did in Bible times. It covers the whole world: the Bible lands and Europe in full detail, the eastern United States more coarsely for the Bible Students, and the rest of the world coarser still (small islands left out) for people of modern times. The coastline is simplified more or less by region but never cut, so no straight seams show. Samoa and the islands just past 180° are repeated east of it, so the South Pacific (Tuvalu, Samoa, Fiji) is not split by the map's edge; the Google Maps link gives their usual longitude. On a map of the whole world the sea names of the Bible lands are left out. `tools/build-basemap.mjs` rebuilds it; its first lines say what to download (pass `-` as the geometry folder to keep the rivers and lakes already in the file). 

People known from history get maps the same way as Bible people: an activity carries a place (`{"p": "<place>"}` as a fourth item in `data/source/history-people.json`) only when its own source names where it happened, so 158 of the 221 history people have maps and the rest (most Ptolemies' and Seleucids' wives, the Herods' children, several emperors, and a few later people whose sources name no place, such as John Wesley and William Miller) have none. Birthplaces the sources give were added as activities (Luther at Eisleben, Russell at Allegheny, Calvin at Noyon, Wycliffe at Oxford, and most Governing Body members). For the Governing Body the places come from their obituaries and life stories in *The Watchtower* and the announcements of new members: where they were born or grew up, where they pioneered or served as missionaries (Kobe, Tuvalu, Samoa, Fiji, Senegal, Ecuador), the Bethel homes at Brooklyn, Wallkill, Patterson and Warwick, and Kingdom Farm at South Lansing, where Gilead School first met. Members still serving are pinned at the world headquarters in Warwick, where the FAQ "What Is the Governing Body of Jehovah's Witnesses?" says they serve. A place the source names only as a country or state (Bohemia, Queensland, Missouri) is drawn as an area, not a point. The 109 places outside the Bible's list (Issus, Gaugamela, Nicaea, Constantinople, Mecca, Medina, Worms, Geneva, Brooklyn, Warwick and others) are at the end of `BIBLE_PLACES` in `data/places.js`, with today's coordinates of the sites. A map whose places are all in Europe or America is never zoomed closer than 9° of latitude, so the coasts stay in view around inland towns. The drawing code is `js/map.js`; the wiring is in `js/app.js`: `mapViews()` draws a person's maps, `syncMap()` keeps the map window on the window in use, `wirePlaces()` and `wireMapBox()` link pins and activities, and `showTip()` draws the event locator.

## Rebuild the people data

After editing `data/characters.js`, `data/source/insight-people.json`, `data/source/history-people.json`, `data/source/activities.json` or `data/source/overrides.json`:

```sh
node tools/build-people.mjs
```

`node tools/build-people.mjs --matches` lists which Insight article each key figure was linked to, so a wrong link (a namesake) is easy to spot and fix in `overrides.json`.

The script merges the curated key figures with the Insight records, links relatives in both directions, finds the Adam-to-Jesus line, fills in placeholder dates and writes `data/people.js` and `data/ASSUMED_DATES.md`. It prints any key figures or line names it could not match.

`data/source/overrides.json` fixes matches by hand. Supported keys:

- `curated`: `{ "key-figure-id": "INSIGHT#N" }` forces a match, or `null` to stop one.
- `drop`: Insight keys to leave out (duplicates such as AMMON, the same man as Ben-ammi, and JEDIDIAH, Solomon's other name).
- `same`: `{ "INSIGHT#N": "INSIGHT#M" }` merges two Insight records of one person into the second, keeping both names (`"MISHAEL#2": "MESHACH"`). Thirteen such pairs are merged, all Insight records of one person under two names: Mishael/Meshach, Saul/Paul, John Mark/Mark, Joseph/Barnabas, Bartholomew/Nathanael, Thaddaeus/Judas, Simon the Zealous One/Simon the Cananaean, Levi/Matthew, Hoshea/Joshua, Hoshea/Hosea, Belteshazzar/Daniel, Chileab/Daniel and Naomi/Mara. Herod the Great's sons Herod Philip, Philip the tetrarch and Herod Antipas each had a second Insight record (under Philip and Antipas), so they are merged too, and Herod's Family row lists each son once.
- `line`, `notLine`: person ids to add to or remove from the Adam-to-Jesus line.
- `tier`: `{ "id": 1-4 }` sets how far you must zoom in before a person appears (1 is always shown).
- `dates`: `{ "id": [born, died] }` replaces a placeholder life span that the generation estimate gets wrong (Ruth's family, for example: Obed is born when Ruth marries Boaz, not a generation later). Jesse is set here too: he must be alive at David's birth (1107 B.C.E.) and while David was a fugitive (1 Samuel 22:3, 4), and his father Obed was born in Ruth's day, so both lives are long.
- `notes`: `{ "id": "text" }` adds a window note to a person who is not a key figure, for example to explain an estimated date.
- `events`: `{ "id": [[year, "text", "scripture", { "p": "place-id" }], ...] }` gives activities to a person who is not a key figure (Joel, Gomer, Belshazzar, Darius the Mede); the place is optional, as in `activities.json`. Their Chinese text goes in the person's `e` list in `data/zh/people.js`, in the same order.
- `unlink`: `{ "id": { "children": ["other-id"] } }` removes a wrong family link in both directions (Drusilla's son Agrippa had been matched to Herod Agrippa I, her father).
- `relNames`: `{ "id": { "children": ["Name"] } }` lists a relative by name only, with no link (Drusilla's son Agrippa, who has no record of his own).

## Activities of minor people

`data/source/activities.json` gives major activities to people who are not key figures, so that a person whose window tells what they did also shows it on their lifespan (Herod Agrippa I, Nicodemus, Laban, the men who rebuilt Jerusalem's wall). It covers about 770 people; with the key figures, history people and `overrides.json`, 1,010 of the 2,815 people now have activities. People known only from a genealogy, or only by an office with nothing they did, have none. The file has four parts:

- `people`: `{ "id": [[year, "text", "scripture", { "c": 1, "p": "place-id", "zh": "Chinese text" }], ...] }`. `c: 1` marks an approximate year, which shows as "c." before the year in the window and the dot's tooltip. `p` is a place id from `data/places.js`, so the activity carries a place tag and a pin on the person's map. `zh` is the Chinese text (no need for a line in `data/zh/people.js`). A string instead of a list names an entry in `shared`.
- `shared`: one activity done by many people together (the men who joined David at Ziklag, those who returned with Zerubbabel in 537 B.C.E., the builders of each part of the wall in Nehemiah 3).
- `dates`: placeholder life spans moved so that they take in an activity (Laban must be alive when Jacob leaves him in 1761 B.C.E.); merged into the `dates` of `overrides.json`.
- `about`: notes on the format.

Years follow the key-figure dates where the activity involves a key figure (Absalom's revolt, Nehemiah's wall), otherwise *Insight on the Scriptures* and the chart of dates in the *New World Translation* (Appendix A6, A7); where the Bible gives no year the activity is placed by the events around it and marked approximate. The texts are short summaries in the site's own words. A place the Bible gives only indirectly is still tagged when the verses show it, and the activity cites them: Philemon lived in Colossae because the congregation met in his house (Philemon 2) and Paul's letter to Colossae calls Onesimus "one of you" and greets Archippus (Colossians 4:9, 17), as *The Watchtower* study article "An Appeal on the Basis of Love" (October 19-25, 2026) also says. The build warns about any activity that falls outside the person's life.
