      About this timeline

      The people of the Bible from Adam to the first-century Christians, and history on to 2025, on one timeline you can zoom and travel through. Pick a topic, or tap a part of the picture above.

      • Drag the timeline to travel through time, and scroll (or pinch on a phone) to zoom. More people appear as you zoom in.
      • The + and − buttons zoom too. The button under them, or the logo, brings back the whole timeline.
      • The bar along the bottom shows all of history at once, with a mark for each person shown. Click or drag on it to jump to a period.
      • Search finds people, events, places, years and verses. Try “Dan 9:25” or “1000 BCE”.
      • The language button switches between English and Chinese, and sets the text size.
      • Hover over a dot (or tap it) to see the person’s portrait, lifespan and family links.
      • Click it (or tap again) to open their window: relatives, events in their life, a map and key scriptures. Click a relative to open theirs.
      • Read more links to the person’s article in Insight on the Scriptures and to chapters about them in Imitate Their Faith or Walk Courageously With God. Every Bible reference opens the verse in the New World Translation.
      • Companions are those who served or traveled with them, such as Elijah and Elisha or Paul and Barnabas. Key figures also list the key figures of their day.
      • Open several windows to compare lives side by side. Fold all and Close all tidy them up at once.
      • The link button copies a link to the person, and the address bar always keeps what is on screen, ready to bookmark or share.

      The people button picks who appears on the timeline:

      EveryoneEvery person named in the Bible, and people from history
      Key figuresThe best-known people and Jesus’ ancestors
      Adam to Jesus lineLuke 3 and Matthew 1, with the mothers the Bible names in it

      Under By job or family you can narrow these to the kings, prophets, wives, daughters and more. Pick several to see anyone in any of them, or switch to all of them for the people in every one, such as the kings who were also prophets.

      Icons after a name show what the person is known for. Someone like David has more than one.

      King / queen Prophet False prophet Patriarch Judge Priest Apostle Official Army commander Bible writer
      • The book button shows the books of the Bible as lines above the main line, each over the years the book covers.
      • Hover over a book (or tap it) to see who wrote it, where and when. Click it to open its own window, which can be moved, folded, bookmarked and shared.
      • Most books end at the diamond; where the writing was finished later, as with Genesis or the Gospels, a dotted line runs on to it. Books with no set period, such as Psalms or the letters, are just the diamond.
      • Where it is crowded, a number such as “+3” stands for several books: hover to list them, or click to zoom in.

      Every birth and death year is one of three kinds:

      From Bible agesWorked out from the ages and reign lengths the Bible gives.
      EstimatedThe Bible gives a clue, such as an age at death, but no exact year.
      AssumedNot recorded, so placed by generation or period. Take it as a rough guide.
      • People and events from history are squares, filled the same way.
      • A large node on the main line is a major event, a turning point in Bible history.
      • c.“c.” before a year means it is approximate.
      • A person’s window has a map of the places the Bible links with them, numbered in the order of their life and joined by the roads and sea routes of the time.
      • Each activity is tagged with its place, and each major event shows where it happened.
      • Zoom with + and −, a pinch or the mouse wheel, and drag to look around. On a computer, drag the map’s corner to make it larger.
      • A dashed pin means the location is uncertain.

      Squares and italic names mark people and events known from history rather than from the Bible. Each links to its source on wol.jw.org, with the scriptures that foretold or mention it.

      1. Flood to SolomonFinds such as the ruins of Ur, Jericho and Hazor, as small squares on people’s lifespans.
      2. Kings and prophetsThe empires around them: the fall of Nineveh, the last kings of Assyria and Babylon, and Persia’s wars with Greece.
      3. Between the TestamentsThe Persian kings after Nehemiah, Alexander the Great, the Greek kings of Egypt and Syria, the Maccabees and Herod’s family.
      4. After ActsHow Jesus’ words about Jerusalem came true: the Roman emperors, the Jewish revolt of 66 C.E. and the fall of Masada in 73.
      5. On to 2025How Christianity changed: Constantine and the church councils, the Bible translators, the Reformation, the Bible Students and the Governing Body of Jehovah’s Witnesses.

      Dates follow Bible chronology, as the sources do, so some are about 20 years earlier than in many history books. People after Bible times show an initial instead of a portrait, because the portraits are drawn in Bible-era dress.
