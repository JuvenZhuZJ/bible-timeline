      About this timeline

      This timeline shows the people of the Bible from Adam to the first-century Christians. Each dot is a person, and each large node is a turning point in Bible history.

      Moving around

      Drag the timeline to move through time, and scroll (or pinch on a phone) to zoom in and out. More people appear as you zoom in. The + and − buttons zoom too, and the button under them brings back the whole timeline, as does clicking the logo. The bar along the bottom shows all of history at once, with a small mark for each person the people button is showing: click or drag on it to jump to a period. To find someone, type their name in the search box. The language button in the top bar switches between English and Chinese.

      Looking at a person

      Hover over a dot (or tap it) to see the person’s portrait, lifespan and family links. Click it (or tap again) to open their window. In a window you can click a relative to open theirs, and click a dot on the lifespan bar to read about something that happened in their life. Bible references open the verse in the New World Translation online. Under Read more, the window links to the person’s article in Insight on the Scriptures and to any chapter about them in Imitate Their Faith or Walk Courageously With God. Key figures also list the other key figures who lived at the same time. The link button at the top of a window copies a link that opens that person, and the address in the browser always keeps what is on screen, so you can bookmark it or send it to someone. Open several windows to compare lives side by side; their lifespans stay under the main line, and Fold all or Close all at the bottom tidies them up at once.

      Who is shown

      The people button in the top bar switches between everyone named in the Bible, the key figures, and only the line from Adam to Jesus (Luke 3:23-38; Matthew 1:1-16). Under By job or family it can also show only the kings and queens, the prophets, the Bible writers, the wives, the daughters and so on. Pick several to see everyone in any of them, or switch to all of them to see only the people who are in every one, such as the kings who were also prophets. Small icons after a name show what the person is known for: a crown for a king or queen, a scroll for a prophet (broken for a false prophet), a staff for a patriarch, scales for a judge, a turban for a priest, a sandal for an apostle, a signet ring for an official, a sword for an army commander and a quill for a Bible writer. Someone known for more than one, such as David, has more than one icon. The Legend lists them.

      Bible books

      The book button in the top bar shows the books of the Bible as lines just above the main line. Each line spans the years the book covers, and the dots on it are main events the book tells about. Hover over a book (or tap it) to see who wrote it, where, and when the writing was completed, with a link to read it online. Click it (or tap again) to open it in its own window, which stays open like a person’s: it can be moved, folded and closed, and its buttons bookmark the book, show it on the band and copy a link to it. Hovering over an event in the window lights up its dot on the band. Click a dot to open that event in the person’s window. A hollow diamond marks the year the writing was completed; an event from that year shows inside it. Most books were finished the year their account ends, so the diamond sits at the end of the line; where the writing was finished later, as with Genesis or the Gospels, a dotted line runs on to it. Books that cover no set period, such as Psalms or the letters, are just the diamond at the year they were completed. Where the band is too crowded to name every book, a small number such as “+3” stands for the books there: hover over it (or tap it) to list them, or click it to zoom in. A Bible writer’s window also lists the books they wrote, and each one opens that book. The years come from the table of the books of the Bible in the New World Translation.

      My notes and bookmarks

      The star at the top of a window bookmarks the person or Bible book, and the My notes box in the window keeps anything you want to write about them. My bookmarks in the people menu shows only the people and books you starred, and My notes and bookmarks lists them all, ready to download or print. They are saved only in this browser on this device; to carry them to another device, download the file and load it there with Load.

      Reading the dates

      Each birth and death year is one of three kinds, and the Legend button shows how each is drawn. A solid dot means the year is worked out from ages and reign lengths the Bible gives. A ring with a small dot means the year is estimated: the Bible gives a clue, such as an age at death, but no exact year. A dashed ring means the year is assumed: the Bible does not record it, so the person is placed by their generation or the period they appear in. Most people have assumed dates, so take their place on the timeline as a rough guide. “c.” before a year means it is approximate.

      Places

      When a window is open, a map shows the places the Bible connects with that person, numbered in the order of their life, with the roads and sea routes between them. Each activity that happened somewhere is tagged with its place, and each major event shows where it happened. On a phone the map sits at the top of the window. A dashed pin means the location is uncertain.

      History between the Testaments

      The Hebrew Scriptures end soon after 443 B.C.E., and Jesus was born in 2 B.C.E. The timeline fills that gap, and the years around it, with people and events known from history rather than from the Bible: the Persian kings after Nehemiah, Alexander the Great, the Greek kings of Egypt and Syria who fought over Judah, the Maccabees and the Hasmonaean rulers, Pompey, and Herod’s family. History also adds the empires around the kings and prophets: the fall of Nineveh, the last kings of Assyria and Babylon, and Persia’s wars with Greece. Dates follow Bible chronology, as the sources on wol.jw.org do, so some are about 20 years earlier than in many history books. After Acts, history also shows how Jesus’ words about Jerusalem came true: the Roman emperors, the Jewish revolt of 66 C.E. and the fall of Masada in 73. They are marked with a square instead of a round dot, both for people and for events on the main line, and an activity from history has a small square beside its source. A square is filled the same way as a dot: solid when the sources give the year, with a small square inside when the year is approximate, and dashed when it is assumed. Each one links to its source on wol.jw.org, mainly Insight on the Scriptures, Pay Attention to Daniel’s Prophecy! and The Watchtower. Where the Bible foretold or mentions something, the scripture is given too, such as Daniel 8 and 11 for the kings of Greece, Egypt and Syria. From history in the people button shows only these people, and the history events appear once you zoom in a little.

      Sources

      Bible references, quotations and name spellings follow the New World Translation of the Holy Scriptures (Study Edition). People and family relationships come from Insight on the Scriptures (Vols. 1 and 2); the summaries are written for this site. Years follow the chronology in Insight on the Scriptures and the charts in the New World Translation, with dates and background from newer study books such as God’s Word Is Truth—A Handbook to the Bible. Locations come from OpenBible.info, checked against Insight on the Scriptures and the New World Translation maps. Portraits are drawings for illustration only.
